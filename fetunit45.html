NO TWO ALIKE

The tyranny of small numbers

Dopants A channel a few tens of nanometers across holds only a few dozen dopant atoms, and their exact number and place are random
Roughness Line edges and metal grains in the gate are never perfectly smooth
Thickness Oxide thickness varies by a fraction of an atomic layer from device to device
Result Two nominally identical transistors side by side have different thresholds
As the device shrinks, the average shrinks with it, and the randomness does not.

PELGROM'S LAW

Bigger devices match better

σ(Vth) = AVT ÷ √(W · L)

The spread in threshold voltage falls with the square root of the gate area, because more dopant atoms average out their random positions. A designer therefore chooses where to spend area: a tiny transistor for density, or a larger one where matching matters, such as in the SRAM cell or an analog pair.

Randomness is the ultimate tax on small transistors.

WHAT IT COSTS

Yield, margins and binning

Memory Vmin An SRAM cell fails if its transistors are mismatched, so the weakest of millions of cells sets the lowest usable supply
Statistics A chip with a billion devices has to tolerate 6σ outliers
Yield Margins and redundancy rescue the chips that would otherwise fail
Binning Chips from one wafer are sorted by speed and sold at different grades
Chip design is partly statistics: make the average good and the worst case survivable.

THE ROADMAP

From planar to stacked

Each generation reshaped the device to keep control as the dimensions fell: planar to FinFET, FinFET to nanosheets, and then stacking the two polarities and powering from the back. Beyond that, 2D channels, tunnel and ferroelectric gates, and new memory are all being explored.

The transistor is still changing shape, not just size.

THREE ROADS

Shrink, add function, or change the switch

More Moore Keep shrinking: nanosheets, CFETs, backside power
More than Moore Add function next to the logic: sensors, radio, power, memory, and stacked chiplets
Beyond CMOS A different kind of switch: nanotubes, tunnel FETs, spintronics
The rest of this course The same MOSFET becomes a sensor: swap the gate for a solution and it reads ions, then proteins and DNA
