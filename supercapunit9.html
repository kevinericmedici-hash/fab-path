THE WISH LIST

What an ideal electrode needs

Electrical conductivity Moves electrons in and out quickly
Large active surface area More places to hold charge
Chemical stability Survives the electrolyte, cycle after cycle
Mechanical durability Doesn't crack, swell, or flake
Electrolyte compatibility Ions can reach and wet the surface
No material maximizes all five — picking one means deciding which to sacrifice.

THE MENU

Three material families

Transition metal oxides

Store charge through fast, reversible redox reactions

Conducting polymers

Store charge through reversible doping and dedoping

Carbon-based materials

Store charge mainly as an electric double layer

The first two are pseudocapacitive; carbon is the workhorse of double-layer storage.
