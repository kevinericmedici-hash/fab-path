A COMPLEMENTARY PAIR

One up, one down

PMOS to the supply Conducts when its gate is low, and pulls the output up to VDD
NMOS to ground Conducts when its gate is high, and pulls the output down to 0 V
Shared gate The same input drives both gates, so exactly one is on
Complementary That is where CMOS gets its name: complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor
Input high gives output low, and input low gives output high. That is a NOT gate.

THE TRANSFER CURVE

Sweep the input

In the two steady states the output sits at a clean 1 or 0, and no current flows from supply to ground because one transistor is always off. Only while the input crosses the middle are both on, and then a brief surge of current flows. The steep part of the curve is where the gate has its gain.

CMOS only spends power when it switches.

RESTORING LOGIC

Noise gets cleaned at every gate

Because the curve is steep in the middle and flat at both ends, a slightly-too-high or slightly-too-low input is pushed to a clean 1 or 0 at the output. The room an input can wander before it is misread is the noise margin. A long chain of gates therefore passes bits without accumulating errors, which an analog signal path cannot do.

Digital logic is reliable because every gate re-draws the bit.

SIZING THE PAIR

Why the PMOS is wider

tpd ≈ C·VDD ÷ Ion
Hole mobility Holes are two to three times slower than electrons in silicon
Equal widths The PMOS pulls up more weakly than the NMOS pulls down, so rises are slower than falls
A wider PMOS Making Wp about 2 to 2.5 times Wn balances the two, and centres the switching point
Delay How fast the output swings is set by the load capacitance and the drive current
