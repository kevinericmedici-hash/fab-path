UNIT 3 • MICROFABRICATION TECHNOLOGY

From ICs to MEMS

Modern MEMS fabrication grew from many of the same technologies used to manufacture integrated circuits.

💻 → ⚙️

Integrated circuits are fabricated by repeatedly adding, modifying, patterning, and removing materials on a silicon wafer.

MEMS uses many of those same fabrication techniques, but extends them to create mechanical structures as well as electronics.

Key idea: MEMS builds on semiconductor manufacturing, but uses those tools to create mechanical structures too.

SILICON WAFERS

Start with the substrate.

Silicon used in microfabrication begins as a large single crystal.

Thin wafers are cut from the crystal and become the substrate on which devices are fabricated.

Raw silicon Starting material
Single crystal Crystal growth
Silicon wafer Device substrate
The wafer is not just a holder. In many MEMS devices, the silicon itself becomes part of the mechanical structure.

SEMICONDUCTORS

What is a semiconductor?

Silicon is a semiconductor whose electrical behavior can be deliberately modified.

Si

By introducing selected impurity atoms into silicon, engineers can change the concentration and type of charge carriers available in the material.

This controlled introduction of impurities is called doping.

DOPING

P-type vs. N-type silicon

P-Type

Group III dopants such as boron increase the concentration of positive charge carriers called holes.

Majority carrier: holes

N-Type

Group V dopants such as phosphorus increase the concentration of mobile electrons.

Majority carrier: electrons
P-type → holes

N-type → electrons

CMOS

What does CMOS mean?

CMOS stands for Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor.

CMOS fabrication creates electronic devices through repeated processing of many patterned layers.

MEMS MATERIALS

Why silicon?

Silicon became an important MEMS material because it combines useful electrical properties with strong mechanical properties and an established fabrication infrastructure.

Electrical

Excellent electrical characteristics and convenient integration with electronics.

Mechanical

High elasticity, high yield strength, low density, and good hardness.

Manufacturing

Well-established microelectronics fabrication methods already exist.

Micromachining

Silicon can be patterned and etched into mechanical structures.

Silicon can serve as both an electronic material and a mechanical material.

MEMS MATERIALS

Silicon by the numbers

Silicon isn't just convenient to fabricate — its mechanical properties rival traditional engineering materials.

Elastic modulus 130–190 GPa (steel ≈ 200 GPa)
Yield strength ≈7 GPa, higher than steel
Density Low, compared to metals
Hardness Comparable to quartz
Crystal structure Single-crystal, diamond cubic lattice

Because single-crystal silicon has no grain boundaries, it deforms almost perfectly elastically, with no hysteresis, and can support resonating structures with a very high quality factor (Q).

Key idea: Silicon combines near-ideal elastic behavior with strength and stiffness on par with steel, at a fraction of the density.

MICROMACHINING

Two major fabrication strategies

Bulk Micromachining

Mechanical features are formed by etching deeply into the silicon substrate.

Surface Micromachining

Structures are built layer by layer on the wafer surface.

Bulk: shape the substrate itself.

Surface: build structures above the substrate.

WAFER-LEVEL PROCESS

Doping

Doping deliberately changes the electrical properties of silicon by introducing selected impurity atoms.

Diffusion

Listed as one semiconductor fabrication method for introducing dopants.

Ion Implantation

Another semiconductor processing method used to introduce dopants.

Doping allows engineers to create regions with different electrical behavior within the same silicon wafer.

THIN-FILM DEPOSITION

Add material where you need it.

Thin-film deposition creates material layers that can later be patterned into functional structures.

CVD / LPCVD

Chemical vapor deposition can form thin films such as polysilicon and other process layers.

Evaporation

A deposition technique commonly used for thin material layers.

Sputtering

Another thin-film deposition method used in microfabrication.

Microfabrication alternates between adding material and removing material.

PATTERN TRANSFER

Optical lithography

Lithography defines where later fabrication operations should occur.

1. Film Material on substrate
2. Photoresist Light-sensitive layer
3. Exposure Pattern through mask
4. Development Create resist pattern
5. Etch Transfer pattern
6. Resist Removal Leave patterned material
Lithography does not usually create the final mechanical structure by itself. It tells later processes where to act.

PHOTORESIST

Positive vs. negative photoresist

Not all photoresists respond to light the same way.

Positive Resist

Exposed regions become soluble and wash away during development. Shipley resist is a common example.

Pattern matches the mask

Negative Resist

Exposed regions become insoluble and remain after development, while unexposed resist washes away. SU-8 is a common example.

Pattern is inverted from the mask
Positive → matches the mask.

Negative → inverts the mask.

MATERIAL REMOVAL

Wet etching vs. dry etching

Wet Etching

Uses liquid chemistry to remove selected material.

Dry Etching

Uses gas or plasma-based processes to remove selected material.

Both appear as important micromachining methods for pattern transfer and silicon material removal.

Choosing how material is removed affects the geometry that can be fabricated.

PROCESS THINKING

Add. Pattern. Remove. Repeat.

Deposit Add a material layer
Pattern Define selected regions
Etch Remove selected material
Modify Oxidize or dope material
Repeat Build complexity layer by layer
This is the mindset you should begin using when looking at any MEMS fabrication sequence:

What material exists now? What should remain? What should be removed? What must be added next?

WORKED EXAMPLE

Inside the cleanroom

Here's how a real device moves through a fabrication run, start to finish.

1. Design & layout CAD layout converted to GDSII, sent to a mask shop
2. Gown up Enter the cleanroom in a bunny suit and gloves
3. Wafer clean RCA clean removes organic and metallic contamination
4. Deposit LPCVD grows nitride, polysilicon, and PSG films
5. Coat & expose Spin-coat photoresist, expose through the mask
6. Etch & strip Pattern is etched into the film, resist is stripped
7. Inspect SEM checks the pattern at high magnification
8. Metallize Au/Cr contacts deposited, then lifted off
This is the same add → pattern → remove cycle you've been learning, run inside a real cleanroom, wafer by wafer.
