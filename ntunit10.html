TWO FAMILIES

How a receptor answers

🚪

Ionotropic

A receptor that is also an ion channel. Binding opens it directly

📡

Metabotropic

A receptor that activates a G protein, starting a signaling cascade inside the cell

One is a door, the other is a doorbell.

SIDE BY SIDE

Direct or relayed

Both start with the transmitter binding to the receptor, but what happens next differs.

Speed is the key difference.

IONOTROPIC

Fast and direct

Speed Responses start within a millisecond
Examples Nicotinic acetylcholine, AMPA and NMDA glutamate, GABA-A, and glycine receptors
Effect Ions flow through the open channel and change the cell's voltage right away
Ionotropic receptors carry the fast signals.

METABOTROPIC

Slower and amplified

Speed Effects take tens of milliseconds to seconds, and can last longer
Examples Dopamine D1 to D5, most serotonin receptors, mGluRs, GABA-B, and muscarinic acetylcholine receptors
Effect One bound receptor can trigger many downstream molecules, amplifying the signal
Metabotropic receptors tune and shape activity.

MANY RECEPTORS

One transmitter, many subtypes

A single transmitter often has many receptor subtypes. Serotonin alone has more than a dozen, and most are metabotropic, though one family is an ion channel. This lets a single molecule do different jobs in different places, and lets drugs target one job at a time.

Selective drugs aim at one subtype.
