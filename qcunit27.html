STACKING

A flip-chip processor

Qubit chip Turned face-down, kept clean.
Wiring chip Carries the control and readout lines.
Bumps Superconducting indium joins the two.
Going vertical separates qubits from wiring.

THE PROBLEM

Running out of edge

Area Qubits grow as n².
Edge Bond pads grow only as n.
The answer Bring signals in vertically.
Two-dimensional wiring can't keep up with qubit counts.

BUMP BONDS

Bonding two chips together

Indium Soft, and superconducting below 3.4 K.
Alignment About a micrometre.
Gap Set by the bump height.
Soft metal makes a superconducting joint.

VIAS

Through-silicon vias

Deep etch The Bosch process drills straight holes.
Coating Superconducting walls, top to bottom.
Two jobs Carry signals and tie grounds together.
Vias turn a chip into a three-dimensional circuit.

BOX MODES

When the chip itself rings

Big chips Ring at lower frequencies.
Danger A mode in the 4 to 8 GHz band steals qubit energy.
The fix Via fences split the chip into small cells.
Big processors need vias and bumps everywhere.
