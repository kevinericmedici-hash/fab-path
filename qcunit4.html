A MAP, NOT A PLACE

The Bloch sphere is a map of a qubit's state

Not where the electron is It doesn't show a position in space. An atom's electron cloud is a different picture.
A map of possibilities Every point on the sphere's surface is one possible state the qubit could be in.
The arrow Points to the qubit's current state, like a pin on a globe.
Named after physicist Felix Bloch, it fits every possible state of one qubit onto a single ball.

THE POLES

North pole = |0⟩, south pole = |1⟩

North pole, |0⟩ The qubit's lower-energy basic state: definitely 0.
South pole, |1⟩ The higher-energy basic state: definitely 1.
On real hardware An atom's ground and excited levels, an electron's spin flipped one way or the other, or a circuit's lowest two energies.
For an electron spin, the arrow even points the way the spin does in space. For other qubits, the sphere is purely a map.

LATITUDE

How far south sets the odds

Near the north pole Likely to read 0.
On the equator Exactly 50/50.
Near the south pole Likely to read 1. Rule: P(0) = cos2(θ/2).
The arrow's height is all you need to know the odds of a measurement.

LONGITUDE

How far around is the phase

The phase The arrow's direction around the vertical axis.
Hidden Measuring directly gives the same odds at every longitude.
But real An H gate turns the phase into a visible difference: interference.
Latitude sets the odds; longitude holds the phase that quantum algorithms steer.
