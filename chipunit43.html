THREE WAYS TO RUN A CHIP COMPANY

Design and build aren't always the same company

design, build, or both

A chip company can design its own chips and build them in its own fabs, design chips and pay someone else to build them, or build chips designed by other companies. Those three choices define an integrated device manufacturer, a fabless company, and a foundry.

Every chip you've ever used came from one of exactly these three arrangements.

THE THREE MODELS

Design, build, or both

🏭

IDM

Designs and builds in its own fabs, such as Intel historically has

💻

Fabless

Designs chips only, such as Nvidia, Qualcomm, or Apple

🔧

Foundry

Builds chips for other companies, such as TSMC or Samsung

A fabless company's whole product never touches a fab it owns.

HOW THE MODELS RELATE

Fabless companies still need someone to build

An IDM handles both design and construction under one roof. A fabless company designs a chip, then ships that design to a foundry, which builds it without ever designing a chip of its own.

A fabless company and a foundry are two halves of what an IDM does alone.

WHY THE SPLIT HAPPENED

Building got too expensive to do casually

fabs got too costly for every design house to own one

Early in the industry, most chip companies were IDMs by default, since there was no other option. As leading-edge fabs grew dramatically more expensive to build, many companies found it cheaper to design chips and pay a foundry to build them than to keep building fabs of their own.

The foundry model exists because building at the leading edge stopped being affordable for most companies.
