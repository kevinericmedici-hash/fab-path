UNIT 2 • CLEANROOM FUNDAMENTALS

Why do we need cleanrooms?

Microfabrication works with extremely small structures, so contamination that seems tiny to us can become a major fabrication defect.

🔬

Dust, fibers, skin particles, oils, chemical residue, and other contaminants can interfere with processes such as lithography, deposition, etching, and bonding.

Key idea: As device features become smaller, contamination control becomes more important.

CONTAMINATION

Where does contamination come from?

🧍

People

Skin particles, hair, clothing fibers, oils, and salts.

🌫️

Airborne Particles

Dust and other particles can settle onto wafer surfaces.

🧪

Process Residue

Chemicals, photoresist, metals, and previous process materials.

A clean wafer can easily become contaminated again if it is handled improperly.

GOWNING

The operator is a contamination source.

Cleanroom clothing helps isolate the wafer from particles produced by the human body.

🥼

Cleanroom Suit

Covers normal clothing and helps contain fibers and particles.

🧤

Gloves

Help prevent oils, salts, and particles from transferring from hands to wafers.

😷

Face & Hair Covering

Reduces contamination from hair, skin, and respiratory droplets.

🥽

Eye Protection

Provides protection while working around chemicals and fabrication equipment.

Cleanroom rule: Protect the wafer from yourself.

CLEANROOM BEHAVIOR

How you move matters.

Fast movement can disturb particles and increase contamination inside the work environment.

✓ Move Carefully

Avoid unnecessary motion around exposed wafers.

✓ Handle Correctly

Use appropriate wafer-handling tools when possible.

✕ Avoid Bare-Hand Contact

Fingerprints contain oils, salts, and particles.

✕ Avoid Unnecessary Items

Personal items can introduce additional particles and contamination.

Good cleanroom behavior is part of the fabrication process itself.

WAFER CLEANING

Start with a clean surface.

💿 ✨

Before important fabrication steps, wafers are cleaned to remove contamination from their surfaces.

Cleaning can target particles, organic residue, and metallic contamination.

One established semiconductor cleaning method is known as RCA cleaning.

A contaminated starting surface can create defects that remain through later processing.

PROCESS FLOW

Fabrication happens in sequence.

Each process step prepares the wafer for the next one.

1. Design Device layout
2. Cleanroom Entry Gown correctly
3. Wafer Cleaning Remove contaminants
4. Deposition Add material
5. Patterning Lithography
6. Etching Transfer pattern
7. Inspection Check the result
The order matters because later processes depend on structures created earlier.

PHOTOLITHOGRAPHY

How are patterns placed on a wafer?

Photolithography transfers a pattern from a photomask onto a wafer.

Photoresist Coat Light-sensitive polymer
Mask Alignment Position the pattern
UV Exposure Expose through mask
Development Create resist pattern
Precise mask alignment is critical because the pattern must appear in the correct location on the wafer.

ETCHING

Transfer the pattern into the material.

▰ → ▱

Once the photoresist has been patterned, selected material can be removed from exposed regions.

Wet Etching

Uses liquid chemical solutions to remove material.

Dry Etching

Uses gas or plasma-based processes to remove material.

During etching, patterned photoresist can act as a protective mask over areas that should remain.

INSPECTION

Inspect before continuing.

Microfabrication is not just process, process, process.

Wafers should be inspected between important fabrication steps so defects can be identified before more processing is performed.

🔬

Optical Microscopy

Useful for routine inspection of patterns, particles, and visible defects.

🖥️

SEM

Scanning electron microscopy provides much higher magnification for examining small fabricated features.

Engineering mindset: If something looks wrong, investigate it before adding another process step.
