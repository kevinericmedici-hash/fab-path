THREE PARTS

Sensor, transmitter, display

〰️

Sensor

A hair-thin filament with its coating, placed just under the skin

📡

Transmitter

Electronics that measure the sensor's current and send it wirelessly

📱

Display

A smartphone app or a dedicated receiver shows the value and trend

In Dexcom G7 the sensor and transmitter are combined into one small disposable.

PUTTING IT ON

A spring-loaded applicator

The user presses an applicator to the skin. A tiny introducer needle carries the filament just under the skin and then withdraws, leaving the filament in place. An adhesive patch holds everything on, and the whole process takes seconds.

The filament is placed once, and the needle does not stay in.

WEAR AND READINGS

How often, and for how long

Readings A new glucose value about every 5 minutes, roughly 288 a day
Wear Sensors last 10 days, and a newer G7 version lasts 15 days
Grace period Dexcom G7 adds a 12-hour grace period after the sensor session ends
Calibration Factory calibrated, so no routine fingersticks are needed
A fresh sensor every 10 days is the routine, so the system makes this step quick.

ALERTS AND SHARING

The CGM watches so you don't have to

Urgent low A fixed alert at 55 mg/dL that cannot be turned off
Low and high Alerts set by the user
Rate of change Alerts when glucose is rising or falling fast
Sharing Readings can be shared with family or caregivers
Insulin pumps Readings can feed an automated insulin delivery system
Alerts are a large part of the value, especially overnight.
