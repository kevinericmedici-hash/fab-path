THE CHECKLIST

Five ingredients for a quantum computer

1. Qubits Well-defined two-level systems you can scale up.
2–3. Reset and coherence Start in 0, and last much longer than a gate.
4–5. Gates and measurement A universal set of gates, and readout of each qubit.
Physicist David DiVincenzo listed these in 2000; every platform is still judged against them.

A REAL QUBIT

Is it a good qubit?

Not enough Having two positions, like a switch or a coin.
What's needed A genuinely quantum two-level system that can hold superpositions.
Good candidates Atoms, electron spins, superconducting circuits, photons.
Quantum behaviour, not just two states, is what makes a qubit.

START FROM ZERO

Reset: every calculation starts at 0

Passive reset Wait for the qubit to relax in a cold chip.
Active reset Measure it, and flip it if it reads 1.
Why cold matters Heat would randomly kick qubits back to 1.
A clean start is the first step of every quantum program.

LIFETIME VS SPEED

Long life, fast gates

Coherence time How long the qubit keeps its quantum state.
Gate time How long one operation takes.
What counts The ratio: how many gates fit before the qubit forgets.
Thousands of gates per lifetime is the minimum for error correction.

CONTROL AND READOUT

Talk to every qubit, one at a time

Control Each qubit needs its own pulses, without disturbing its neighbours.
Readout Fast, accurate measurement of each qubit.
At scale Thousands of control lines are a major engineering challenge.
Every one of these ingredients turns into a fabrication and wiring problem later in the course.
