TWO QUBITS

Two qubits, one shared state

Four amplitudes |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩ and |11⟩.
Not entangled The state splits into 'A's state' times 'B's state'.
Entangled It can't be split: only the pair has a state.
Entanglement is a property of the whole, not of the parts.

THE RECIPE

Making a Bell pair

H Puts qubit A in an equal blend.
CNOT Flips B only when A is 1.
Result (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2: always 00 or 11.
Two gates are enough to entangle two qubits.

MATCHING RESULTS

Perfectly linked, perfectly random

Each qubit alone Gives 0 or 1 at random.
The pair Always agrees, however far apart.
No messages Because each side looks random, nothing can be sent faster than light.
Linked results, but no signal: that's entanglement.

THE BELL TEST

Stronger than any classical link

The question Could the particles just carry hidden instructions?
The test Measure at different angles and compute a score S.
The answer Hidden instructions give at most 2; nature reaches about 2.83.
Experiments confirming this won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics.

WHY IT MATTERS

Computers need entanglement

Power Without entanglement a quantum computer is no better than a classical one.
Error correction Spreads one qubit's information across many entangled qubits.
Fragility Entanglement is easily destroyed, and it can spread errors too.
