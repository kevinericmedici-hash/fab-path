CAREER PATHS

Chip careers, mapped to what you can learn.

The semiconductor industry hires for far more than one job title. Pick a role to see what it involves and which Fab Path units build the foundations for it.

Process engineer

Owns the steps that build a chip (deposition, etch, lithography, cleans) and tunes them for yield and repeatability.

LEARN IT HERE

  • Cleanroom Fundamentals
  • Microfabrication Technology
  • Game: Match the Process

Device engineer

Connects process choices to electrical behavior: threshold voltage, drive current, leakage, and reliability.

LEARN IT HERE

  • MOSFET Fundamentals
  • Semiconductors, Doping & CMOS

Equipment & tool engineer

Keeps the lithography scanners, etchers, and deposition chambers running: vacuum, plasma, motion control, and uptime.

LEARN IT HERE

  • Cleanroom Fundamentals
  • Microfabrication Technology
  • Game: Spot the Equipment

Circuit & RF design engineer

Designs the circuits on the chip using a foundry's transistor models, so gain, noise, and bias have to be intuitive.

LEARN IT HERE

  • MOSFET Fundamentals: I-V, gm, subthreshold
  • Capacitive sensing & readout

Packaging & test engineer

Turns finished dies into shipped parts: dicing, die attach, wire and flip-chip bonding, encapsulation, and test.

LEARN IT HERE

MEMS & sensor engineer

Builds microscale sensors and actuators such as accelerometers, micromirrors, and microfluidic chips.

Where these roles exist

Foundries, integrated device makers, equipment suppliers, fabless design companies, packaging and test houses, national labs, and university cleanrooms all hire for these functions.

  • Foundries (e.g. TSMC, GlobalFoundries)
  • Device makers (e.g. Intel, Texas Instruments)
  • Equipment (e.g. ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research)
  • Fabless design (e.g. Qualcomm, NVIDIA)
  • Packaging & test houses
  • National labs & university cleanrooms

An honest scope note. Fab Path teaches the fundamentals underneath these roles. It is not company-specific training, it doesn't cover any company's proprietary processes, and advanced-node topics such as EUV lithography are not covered.
