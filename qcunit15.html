ZERO RESISTANCE

Cool a wire, lose the resistance

Ordinary metals Electrons scatter, so some resistance always remains.
Superconductors Below a critical temperature Tc, the resistance is exactly zero.
1911 Heike Kamerlingh Onnes saw it first, in mercury at 4.2 K.
Zero resistance means a circuit can hold energy without losing it.

PERSISTENT CURRENT

A current that never stops

Copper ring The current fades away as heat.
Superconducting ring The current flows on with no measurable decay.
For qubits Energy sloshing around a circuit isn't lost to resistance.
A lossless circuit is the starting point for a qubit.

MEISSNER EFFECT

Superconductors push out magnetic fields

Expulsion Below Tc, surface currents cancel the field inside.
Levitation A magnet can float above a superconductor.
Trapped flux Vortices trapped in the film absorb energy, so chips need magnetic shields.
Superconductivity is more than zero resistance: it's a new state of matter.

MATERIALS

Which metals, and how cold?

Aluminium 1.2 K: the junction metal in nearly every superconducting qubit.
Nb, Ta, TiN Higher Tc; used for pads, resonators and wiring.
Ceramics Superconduct in liquid nitrogen, but are too lossy for qubits.
Qubit chips are made from a handful of well-understood metals.

WHY SO COLD?

Why 10 millikelvin?

Qubit energy At 5 GHz, hf/kB is only about 0.24 K.
Heat flips qubits The share kicked into 1 is about e−hf/kBT.
10 to 20 mK Cold enough that almost every qubit starts in 0.
The fridge must be far colder than the metal needs, for the qubit's sake.
