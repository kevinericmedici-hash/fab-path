PULL-UP AND PULL-DOWN

Two networks, one output

Pull-up network PMOS transistors between VDD and the output
Pull-down network NMOS transistors between the output and ground
Duals Where one network has transistors in series, the other has them in parallel
Never both For every input, exactly one network conducts: the output is always driven
Every static CMOS gate is a pull-up network facing its dual pull-down network.

BUILD A GATE

Series is AND, parallel is OR

In a NAND gate the output goes low only when both NMOS are on, so they sit in series, and the PMOS in parallel. A NOR gate is the mirror image. Any logic function can be built from NAND gates alone, which is why NAND is called universal.

Flip the inputs and watch which network lights up.

WIRE THE NETWORKS

Series or parallel?

Series means AND, parallel means OR, and the two networks are duals.

WHERE THE POWER GOES

Dynamic plus static

P = α·C·VDD²·f + VDD·Ioff

Each charge-up draws an energy C·VDD² from the supply: half is stored on the load and half is lost as heat in the PMOS, and the discharge then turns the stored half into heat in the NMOS. So dynamic power scales with the square of the voltage and linearly with frequency and activity. Leakage adds a floor that does not depend on switching at all.

Voltage is the strongest lever: halve it and dynamic power falls to a quarter.

THE TRADE

Slower, cooler, or both

Lower VDD Cuts dynamic power as V², but slows the gates, since the drive current falls
Lower activity Clock gating stops idle blocks from switching at all
Lower C Smaller transistors and shorter wires switch less capacitance
Dynamic voltage and frequency scaling Chips lower VDD and f together when the workload is light
Every mobile processor is a continuous negotiation between speed and the battery.
