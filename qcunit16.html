PAIRING

Two electrons become a pair

The lattice A moving electron pulls the positive ions together.
Attraction A second electron is drawn to that positive trail.
Cooper pair Two electrons acting as one particle of charge 2e.
Through the lattice, electrons that normally repel can attract.

ONE WAVE

One giant quantum wave

Above Tc Unpaired electrons with random phases.
Below Tc Pairs share one phase and move in step.
Macroscopic One quantum state spread across a whole circuit.
That's how a circuit you can see can behave like an atom.

THE GAP

It costs energy to break a pair

Gap 2Δ About 3.5 kBTc: roughly 90 GHz for aluminium.
Qubit photons 4 to 8 GHz: far too weak to break pairs.
Low loss Microwaves slosh around without being absorbed.
The gap protects the qubit's energy from the metal itself.

QUASIPARTICLES

When pairs break anyway

Causes Stray infrared light, heat and cosmic rays.
Effect Quasiparticles tunnel through the junction and cause errors.
Defences Shields, filters and quasiparticle traps.
Even at 10 mK, a few broken pairs can limit a qubit.

FLUX QUANTUM

Flux comes in packets

Φ0 = h/2e About 2.07 × 10−15 webers.
Proof of pairs Its value showed in 1961 that pairs carry the current.
For qubits Flux through loops is measured in units of Φ0.
Quantum rules show up in a ring you could hold in your hand.
