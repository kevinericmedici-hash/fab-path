THE CHANDELIER

Inside a dilution refrigerator

Plates 50 K, 4 K, 0.8 K, 0.1 K and 10 mK.
Wiring Coax lines with attenuators on each plate.
At the bottom The chip, inside a magnetic shield.
Each plate is colder than the one above it.

COOLDOWN

From room temperature to 10 mK

Pulse tube Pre-cools the plates to about 50 K and 3 K.
Dilution Takes the bottom plate to about 10 mK.
Time Typically one to two days.
Modern 'dry' fridges need no bath of liquid helium.

THE TRICK

How dilution cooling works

Two layers Below 0.87 K the helium mixture separates.
Crossing Helium-3 crossing the boundary absorbs heat.
A cycle The still pumps it out and it's recycled.
A continuous cycle that can run for months.

COOLING POWER

Every wire costs cooling power

Q̇ ∝ T² Cooling power falls steeply as it gets colder.
Microwatts That's all there is to spend at 10 mK.
Scaling Thousands of wires means a heat problem.
Wiring is one of the hardest problems in scaling up.

ATTENUATION

Keep room-temperature noise out

Thermal noise About 1,000 photons per mode at 300 K and 6 GHz.
Attenuators Absorb noise on each plate.
Target Below 0.001 photons at the chip.
The fridge's wiring is designed as carefully as the chip.
